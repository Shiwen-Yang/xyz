## 1. Synopsis and objectives

This is a phase 2, randomized, double-blind, three-arm study of Fictional Product A in adults aged 18–65 with the imaginary condition Q. The two primary objectives compare each active dose with placebo on change in Q score and Q response at week 12. Both endpoints must succeed for a dose to be declared successful. Q response is an important binary measure; the two endpoint definitions below are co-primary. The secondary objective compares change in Q score at week 24. Planned accrual is 300 subjects, 100 per arm; approximately 270 are expected to contribute week-12 measurements.

## 2. Design

Randomization is 1:1:1, stratified by region (North/South). Subjects, investigators, and outcome assessors are blinded. The dispensing pharmacist has allocation access before database lock. Fictional regimens are low-dose Product A (1 unit orally daily), high-dose Product A (2 units orally daily), and matching placebo daily for 12 weeks. There are 12 sites: 8 in the US and 4 in Canada. The expected US enrollment percentage is not specified. No additional unusual design feature is planned.

## 3. Endpoints and hypotheses

Primary endpoint SCORE is baseline-to-week-12 change in Q score, calculated as week-12 score minus baseline score; higher scores are better. Primary endpoint RESPONSE is an increase of at least 5 Q-score points from baseline at week 12. The secondary endpoint is baseline-to-week-24 change in Q score.

For each active dose versus placebo, the score hypothesis is H0: mean change difference = 0 versus H1: mean change difference is not 0. The response hypothesis is H0: response-probability difference = 0 versus H1: response-probability difference is not 0. Differences are active minus placebo. There is no non-inferiority or equivalence margin. Four co-primary comparisons (two doses by two endpoints) are each tested at two-sided alpha 0.0125 using Bonferroni allocation from family alpha 0.05. Both endpoint tests must be significant for a dose to succeed. Week-24 analyses are exploratory without a confirmatory alpha allocation.

## 4. Analyses

All primary and secondary analyses use all randomized subjects, analyzed as randomized (ITT). SCORE uses ANCOVA with treatment, region, and baseline Q score; RESPONSE uses a region-stratified CMH test. Missing week-12 Q scores are multiply imputed under a missing-at-random assumption using treatment, region, and baseline Q score. Response is derived from each imputed score. Sensitivity analyses use a pattern-mixture model for SCORE and treat missing responses as nonresponse for RESPONSE. Prespecified exploratory subgroups for both endpoints are age (18–40/>40), sex, and race.

The week-24 analysis uses ANCOVA with treatment, region, and baseline Q score, with multiple imputation under missing at random. An observed-case ANCOVA is supportive. Week-24 subgroup analyses use the same groups.
